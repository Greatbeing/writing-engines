Atom library

Lay the engines side by side and many moves turn out to be the same move. An atom is one such move: small enough to practise, general enough to recombine. Different authors are different combinations of the same atoms.

182 atoms · 18 shared families · 63 unique · 17 engines

Names and one-line definitions are given in English. Each atom’s full page is in Chinese.

Cold Sketch13 · 冷笔白描

Shared across 7 engines.

  • Chores in Full (Chinese)Li Juan
    Write out the steps, quantities and feel of a small task one by one; never summarize.
  • Landscape Sketch (Chinese)Li Juan
    Write sky, snow and grass in nouns and verbs, exact in color and shape, with no big words.
  • Family Comedy (Chinese)Li Juan
    Sketch what family members do and say without judging; let them be funny, with tenderness inside.
  • Cold Sketch (Chinese)Lu Xun
    Feeling stays unspoken, and the coldest line is the last one.
  • Craft in Detail (Chinese)Wang Zengqi
    Get a craft's tools, steps, timing and jargon exactly right, without praise.
  • Plants by Name (Chinese)Wang Zengqi
    Name the plants, give their look, smell, habitat and use, and don't make them carry feeling.
  • Zero-Degree Narration (Chinese)Yu Hua
    Tell the heaviest things in the calm voice of small talk or bookkeeping; the narrator never judges.
  • Death Written Lightly (Chinese)Yu Hua
    A death gets a sentence or two; the next sentence is an everyday action.
  • Cold Accounting (Chinese)Eileen Chang
    Write out the calculations inside human ties calmly, like keeping books, without blame.
  • How People Eat (Chinese)Ah Cheng
    Write eating as a solemn series of actions: not whether it tastes good, only how it is eaten.
  • Action for Thought (Chinese)Ah Cheng
    Don't write what people think or feel, only what their hands, eyes and bodies do.
  • Situated Detail (Chinese)Renwu Feature
    Objects, weather and work gestures are written into the person's situation, each with a source.
  • Restrained Feeling (Chinese)Renwu Feature
    Pain and emotion come only from the pressure of facts, without one lyrical word.

Shared across 10 engines.

  • Scene Hook (Chinese)Zhejiang Commentary
    Give an abstract claim body heat with one specific person, image or detail.
  • Small Entry (Chinese)Lu Xun
    Start from something too small for anyone to have thought of writing about.
  • Small to Large (Chinese)People's Daily
    Bring a grand theme down to one person, one village, one workshop.
  • Observational Opening (Chinese)Stand-up
    Open on a small detail everyone has lived through but no one has said.
  • Occasion Opening (Chinese)Su Shi
    One sentence gives time, place and occasion, then straight in.
  • Personal Anecdote Opening (Chinese)Wang Xiaobo
    Begin with a small thing "I" went through, as if talking to a friend.
  • Leisurely Opening (Chinese)Wang Zengqi
    Don't state the theme; start casually from a place, a thing, or an idle remark.
  • Micro-Scene (Chinese)Sanlian
    In about thirty characters, a scene with a person, a line and a turn.
  • Thing-First Opening (Chinese)Ah Cheng
    The first sentence is an event, a scene or a place, with no historical setup.
  • Opening Frame (Chinese)Renwu Feature
    Open on one concrete image or moment, putting the reader on the scene.

  • Borrowed Jargon (Chinese)Sanlian

Shared across 8 engines.

  • Stop on an Action (Chinese)Li Juan
    Don't sum up or elevate; stop on an action, a line, or an image.
  • Unresolved Ending (Chinese)Lu Xun
    No elevation, no solution: leave a cold line or a question.
  • Playful Close (Chinese)Wang Xiaobo
    No elevation or rallying: follow the reasoning to a light conclusion or a self-joke.
  • Set It Down Lightly (Chinese)Wang Zengqi
    No summing up: stop on a small matter, an idle remark, or an image.
  • Quiet Ending (Chinese)Yu Hua
    Return to an everyday image, a homely line, or an action, without elevation.
  • Relaxed Close (Chinese)Sanlian
    No slogans, no verdict: land back in a concrete scene of daily life.
  • Cold Close (Chinese)Ah Cheng
    Stop on an ordinary action or line, without summary or sigh.
  • Faint Close (Chinese)Versailles
    End on a sigh or a tired face, never saying it outright.

    Sketch "a person like this" in a few strokes, naming no one, so everyone fits.

    Sketch a wrong way of working, give it a memorable name, describe the conduct, name no one.
  • Positive Model (Chinese)Mao Zedong

  • A Few Strokes (Chinese)Wang Zengqi

  • Group as Person (Chinese)Sanlian

  • Sample Profile Formula (Chinese)Sanlian
    Tag plus name plus role plus one line in their own words.
  • One Line Makes a Person (Chinese)Ah Cheng
    A character enters with one line or one habit, stands, and is never explained.

  • Modesty with a Catch (Chinese)Versailles

Reversal6 · 反转揭底

Shared across 5 engines.

  • The Reveal (Chinese)Jin Yong
    At the height of conflict, turn up the truth and rearrange everyone's position.
  • Setup and Punchline (Chinese)Stand-up
    One line builds an expectation; the next overturns it in as few words as possible.
  • Reversal (Chinese)Stand-up
    Flip the whole logic of a bit, swapping identity or cause and effect.
  • Reverse Sympathy (Chinese)Eileen Chang
    Having seen through someone's small scheme, turn back with a line on their behalf.
  • False Consensus (Chinese)Sanlian
    Say "everyone feels this way", then take it apart yourself.
  • Complaint as Boast (Chinese)Versailles
    Complain about the "faults" of good things: too big, too many, too fast.

Unique63 · 独有

Moves found in one engine only.

  • Acknowledge (Chinese)Zhejiang Commentary
    First, grant that what the reader feels right now is legitimate.
  • Sentiment Arbitrage (Chinese)Zhejiang Commentary
    Topic choice: collective feeling building up, times a moment no authority has answered.
  • Peer Positioning (Chinese)Zhejiang Commentary
    Treat readers as people thinking it through with you, not people being taught.
  • Fight Storyboard (Chinese)Jin Yong
    Cut a fight into shots: who is where, what move, why, and what follows.
  • Planted Secret (Chinese)Jin Yong
    Show early that a character is withholding something, and keep it withheld.
  • Ensemble of Factions (Chinese)Jin Yong
    Several parties share a scene; a few strokes each show their separate calculations.
  • Loyalty Dilemma (Chinese)Jin Yong
    Make a character choose between two right things, and pay for it.
  • Historical Setting (Chinese)Jin Yong
    Ground the story in a real reign or war, with private feuds under the larger tide.
  • Couplet Chapter Titles (Chinese)Jin Yong
    Write chapter titles as a matched couplet, each half pointing to one plot turn.
  • Distinct Voices (Chinese)Jin Yong
    Everyone speaks differently; hide the names and you still know who is talking.
  • Naive Eye (Chinese)Li Juan
    The narrator plays the one who doesn't understand, seeing things as if for the first time.
  • One Light Sigh (Chinese)Li Juan
    When feeling peaks, press it into one ordinary sentence, then return to chores.
  • Straight to the Point (Chinese)Mao Zedong
    The first sentence states the problem or the thesis, with no detour.
  • High Keynote (Chinese)People's Daily
    In the first paragraph, place the issue on the national and historical map and give the overall judgment.
  • Time Coordinates (Chinese)People's Daily
    Mark where "now" sits with dates or stages, explaining why it is said now.
  • Act-out (Chinese)Stand-up
    Don't describe it; perform the character, the object, the inner voice.
  • Joy in Hardship (Chinese)Su Shi
    Describe a bad situation honestly first, then find something in it to enjoy.
  • A Turn of Thought (Chinese)Su Shi
    Write being too tired to go on, then one thought clears it and it is set down.
  • Things Have Owners (Chinese)Su Shi
    Admit most fine things aren't yours, and prize the free ones anyone may enjoy.
  • See for Yourself (Chinese)Su Shi
    Neither believe nor dismiss hearsay; go and look first.
  • The Interest Test (Chinese)Wang Xiaobo
    Treat "is it interesting, does it make you think" as a serious standard.
  • The Silent Majority (Chinese)Wang Xiaobo
    Join no loud side; just say clearly what you saw.
  • Explaining Things (Chinese)Wang Zengqi
    Like a knowing friend, say what a thing is called, its local names, how it's made and eaten.
  • The Digression (Chinese)Wang Zengqi
    Wander to another place, year or thing, then return without a seam.
  • Customs Laid Out (Chinese)Wang Zengqi
    Lay out a place's seasons, rules and households first; people and events grow out of them.
  • Short Spoken Lines (Chinese)Wang Zengqi
    Short sentences that read aloud; a long one is followed by a very short one.
  • Classical Touches (Chinese)Wang Zengqi
    Slip in a familiar classical phrase or old name, no more than three times in a piece.
  • Dialogue Drives Plot (Chinese)Yu Hua
    Short, plain lines go back and forth and move the story; the only tag is "said".
  • Frame Narrator (Chinese)Yu Hua
    An outsider "I" listens to someone tell their own life.
  • Mind Through Objects (Chinese)Eileen Chang
    Leave the feeling unsaid; show how someone handles an object.
  • A Touch of Color (Chinese)Eileen Chang
    One or two colors, fastidiously exact, set the emotional key.
  • Common Things (Chinese)Eileen Chang
    Write vegetable prices, power bills and frozen dumplings with relish, never disdain.
  • Desolate Undertone (Chinese)Eileen Chang
    In a lively or trivial moment, add one stroke of time, leaving or loss, and it turns cold.
  • Sound and Light Transitions (Chinese)Eileen Chang

  • Meme Archaeology (Chinese)Sanlian
    Take an online phrase as the topic: trace its source, who uses it, and the mindset behind it.
  • Pop-Science Seasoning (Chinese)Sanlian

  • Pun (Chinese)Sanlian
    One word with two meanings, used as a headline-grade joke.
  • Plain Dialogue (Chinese)Ah Cheng
    Very short exchanges, mostly without "he said", revealing the measure between people.
  • Folk Wisdom (Chinese)Ah Cheng
    Let an ordinary person state a plain truth in homely words; the narrator doesn't respond.
  • Classical Bones (Chinese)Ah Cheng

  • Heavy Things Lightly (Chinese)Ah Cheng
    The heaviest event gets the fewest words, sometimes only a trace left after.
  • The Way in the Common (Chinese)Ah Cheng
    Set the most common thing beside something of the Way, and say neither is higher.
  • In Passing (Chinese)Versailles
    Tuck the noun being shown off into an adverbial or a clause; the main clause is about something small.
  • Praise by Proxy (Chinese)Versailles
    The praise comes from someone else; you only pass it on, helplessly.
  • Brands Dropped Lightly (Chinese)Versailles
    Refer to brands, schools and places by abbreviation, color or nickname, like old friends.
  • Asking for Advice (Chinese)Versailles

  • Feigned Unfamiliarity (Chinese)Versailles
