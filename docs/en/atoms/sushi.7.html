Atoms · Su Shi · sushi.7

Things Have Owners

SignatureFamily · Unique

Signature: Su Dongpo's view of what to take and what to leave; tilt it slightly and it becomes resentment of the rich or false high-mindedness.

In one line: Admit most fine things aren't yours, and prize the free ones anyone may enjoy.

Why it works

Admit first that you can't have it, and the reader doesn't think you're putting on airs. Then point to good things close at hand that cost nothing, and the reader's sense of lack is caught by a specific substitute. It doesn't ask people to give up desire; it just offers another way out.

From the source

惟江上之清风，与山间之明月，耳得之而为声，目遇之而成色。(Former Ode on the Red Cliff)

Translation: Only the cool breeze on the river and the bright moon among the hills: the ear takes in the one and it becomes sound; the eye meets the other and it becomes colour.

Taking it apart

  • "Only": the text has just said "if a thing is not mine, I will not take even a hair of it"; here one word turns the direction.
  • "Cool breeze" and "bright moon": things no one can own.
  • "The ear takes in" and "the eye meets": it lands on the senses; the enjoyment is concrete, not a slogan.

Overdone

So what about mansions and fancy cars? The rich aren't necessarily happier than me. True wealth is richness of the heart.

The problem: it runs others down first, then consoles itself. Resentment of the rich plus a motivational quote, and not one specific thing that costs nothing.

When not to use it

  • When the reader lacks basic security in life: telling them to enjoy the sunset sounds glib.
  • When the piece is really meant to criticise unfair distribution: this atom would dissolve the problem.

Five-minute exercise

Write about something you can't afford, admitting it in just one sentence. Then write about something you enjoyed for free today, using two sensory details.

Self-check

Is there any line belittling "those who have"? Is the free thing concrete enough to be felt through the senses?
