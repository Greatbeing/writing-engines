## 2. Reconstructing the atom chain

Small to Large ×2 → High Keynote → Rising Parallels (three-part argument, one Small to Large per section) → Couplet Close

Five or six atoms in all, inside the People's Commentary rule of "four to six" combinations: the standard route of "start from one thing, fix the key with one sentence." But Dialectical Reply is missing; the ending jumps straight from the cases to the couplet, and so does not satisfy the rule that "a Dialectical Reply must come before the Call to Action."

## 3. The six-item style fingerprint check

1. **Keynote in the first paragraph**: pass. A People's Commentary may open on a single thing, and paragraph 4 (in fact the second argument paragraph) gives the overall judgment.
2. **Alternating large and small**: pass, and this is the piece's strongest point. Six Small to Large moves in the whole text, and within three sentences after each sub-argument it lands on a specific place name.
3. **Vertical and horizontal together**: fail. There is no historical depth; the only figure is "a hundred-plus" and it has no source; the cases rest entirely on "two media reports."
4. **Visible rhythm**: pass. The three "from... comes" subheads form Rising Parallels; Couplet Close appears once at the head and once at the tail; the four-character phrases appear in groups, never more than four in a row.
5. **Argues without lecturing**: half pass. No lecturing sentence patterns; but the last third has only one line, "we must not fall into the habit of dealing only with the case in hand," which does not amount to a Dialectical Reply.
6. **Grounded landing**: basically pass. The closing couplet is drawn out of the earlier cases, but it lands on "a moving sight," so the call is a little soft.

Two items fail (vertical and horizontal together; argues without lecturing); by the fingerprint rule this counts as "the process was not walked to the end."

## 4. Suggested fixes

1. **Add one Dialectical Reply to section 3**: after the "rider map" case, openly admit one layer of difficulty — fine-grained governance costs money, grassroots staffing is limited, and "one circle, one policy" can turn into "one circle, one burden" — then give the answer from a higher level (a long-term mechanism is exactly what makes fine-grained work sustainable). Only then does the Call to Action have a road behind it.
2. **Add one set of traceable figures**: for example national coverage of convenient living circles 【待核：authoritative release from the Ministry of Commerce and others】, placed after the High Keynote in paragraph 4, so that the judgment "from whether we have it to whether it is good" has data to weigh it down.
