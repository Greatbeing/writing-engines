# The Exercise Book

*Translated from the Chinese; the techniques are annotated where English cannot carry them.*

In the second year my mother lay paralysed in bed, my younger brother and I began to keep accounts.

We kept them in an exercise book, one my nephew had used up. On the cover were the two characters for "Mathematics", and under them, Class Two, Grade Three. The first pages were my nephew's addition and subtraction, more than half of it wrong, covered in dense red crosses. My brother said, "We'll start our accounts after his mistakes end. For luck." I said a mistake and luck in the same breath. He said once the mistakes are all used up it is time to be right.

The first page was mine: March 4th, Elder Brother, one sponge bath, three diapers changed, two meals fed, medicine 18.50.

The second page was my brother's: March 5th, Number Two, one sponge bath, four diapers changed, three meals fed, medicine 18.50, bananas one jin 3.20. [Note: they sign themselves 大哥 and 老二, "elder brother" and "number two", the names the household uses rather than given names; 娘, which the text uses for the mother, is the old country word. A jin is 500 grams.]

I looked at it and said, "You fed her one extra meal?"

"She was hungry in the night," he said.

"Bananas go in too?"

"The bananas were for Mother."

"You ate two of them yourself."

"Then I'll put down one jin. What I actually bought was a jin and a half."

After that we kept it day by day, one of us on odd dates, one on even. A diaper was 1.20, later it went up to 1.50, and my brother drew an arrow in the margin and wrote, "Price went up, not my account." The medicine money went from 18.50 to 26, then to 31. We split everything half and half; at the end of the month we did the sums, and I had paid 47 yuan more. He said next month he would pay more. I said you won't pay more next month. He said then the month after.

My mother did not know we were keeping accounts. She thought we were checking my nephew's homework. Once she said, "How does this child get so much homework? A page a day." My brother said, "Mother, his marks are bad, the teacher is punishing him." My mother sighed and said, just like you when you were little.

My brother really was bad when he was little. He admits it himself. He says that in his whole life he has not got right as many maths problems as are in this exercise book.

The second exercise book was Chinese. The third was English, with a foreign child laughing on the cover. My brother said this one was not suitable for accounts, foreigners would not understand Chinese diapers. I said then don't look at the cover.

In the winter of the third year my brother's back went, he could not bend, he could not do the sponge baths. He wrote in the exercise book: December 9th, Number Two, sponge baths nil, diapers changed nil, three meals fed, two hours sitting and talking. Below that, in small characters: back's no good, owe elder brother eleven sponge baths, repay in spring.

When spring came he repaid nine. With two still owing, my mother went.

That day was my odd date. I went in the morning, gave her a sponge bath, changed one diaper, fed her half a bowl of gruel; she ate three mouthfuls and said she was full. At noon my brother came to take over, and she was not eating any more.

My brother sat on the edge of the bed with the exercise book, turned to the last page, and asked me, "Whose account is today?"

I said mine, the morning was me.

He said the afternoon was his. So he wrote: April 2nd, Number Two, sat with her all afternoon.

Afterwards we went to arrange the funeral, and that money was split half and half too. When it was done I took out the three exercise books and added everything up from the beginning to the end. Three years and one month: more than two thousand six hundred diapers, ten thousand four hundred yuan of medicine, and I had paid one hundred and twelve yuan more than he had.

My brother said, "I still owe you two sponge baths."

I said, "I don't want them."

He said, "And the one hundred and twelve yuan?"

"That either."

He thought for a moment, took the exercise book, and wrote on the last line: settled in full. Having written it he said, these are the two most correct characters I have written in my life. [Note: he writes 两清, literally "two clear", both accounts even; calling them two characters rather than two words is the point, and that point does not survive the alphabet.]

The three exercise books are in my nephew's hands now. He is in middle school, and he uses them for rough paper; on the back he works out equations, and more than half of them are still wrong.
