## 1. Which techniques it uses (atoms)

The whole piece strings together four entries from “City Vitality 100”: pets on campus, Maoxiang Village’s pet village, the Jinjiang River dredging that kept a bird island, and bird-watching residents in Luohu.

- **Atom 1, False Consensus**: it opens “Who is the city built for? In the past, this question had only one answer,” first setting up a consensus everyone takes for granted, then dismantling it.
- **Atom 5, Micro-Scene**: the Jinjiang section is the best — “when people come close, it flies off” — a person, a bird, an action, and the turn completed within 30 characters.
- **Atom 3, Three Concrete Things**: the campus adds “signposts, poop bags, water points and fences”; the bank is newly planted with “thalia, soft rush, water canna.” Concrete things stand in for abstract words like “supporting facilities” or “greening.”
- **Atom 8, Doubt and Rebuttal**: the Maoxiang Village section first voices the dog owners’ grievances for them (parks ban pets, malls require them to be bagged, lawns mean ticks), then gives the answer.
- **Atom 10, Ring Structure**: the Jinjiang section opens with an egret “waiting for fish” and ends with the egret “not flying off even when someone walks by” — a small, clean ring.
- **Atom 19, Then and Now Couplet**: the ending, “the city no longer asks only ‘is it convenient for people,’” sets the old question against the new one.

The numbers are solid: Maoxiang Village’s collective income “rose from 18,000 yuan to over 1 million yuan,” 9 more bird species, overwintering waterbirds up about 14.7%.

## 2. The 3 places that most read like AI

1. **The whole third paragraph of the opening**: after “the city is loosening from a spatial logic centered on people” come three “is changing” lines in a row, all abstract judgments — about 150 characters without a single image. Opinion sentences placed at the head of each paragraph, every paragraph explaining “why”: it steps right on the red line.
2. **The commentary paragraph in the pets-on-campus section**: “not only lets young people’s emotional companionship continue, but can also to a great extent reduce…” — “not only… but also” plus “to a great extent” is classic written-register connective tissue. It reads like a policy explainer, not like a person talking.
3. **The ending lands on emotion**: the last sentence comes to rest on two adjectives, “softer, more alive,” a lyrical-essay close. And the whole piece has **zero** people samples — the students, dog owners and bird-watching residents have no names and no quoted words; all four entries are propped up by what institutions did.

## 3. How to fix it (2 points)

1. **Add 1 people sample to the pets-on-campus section**: it must come from a real interview, for example a first-year student at Kunming City College, in the form “tag + name + scene + own words,” with one scene of them doing the paperwork for a pet-friendly dorm, replacing the “not only… but also” commentary line in point 2. If the material can’t be gathered, delete the commentary line; don’t fabricate one.
2. **Replace the last sentence of the ending**: swap “softer, more alive” for a concrete scene — the Jinjiang section already has one ready: the egret on the shallows that doesn’t fly off when someone walks by. Land back on that bird, then add one relaxed punchline; that carries more of a sense of a living person [活人感, “live-person feel”, this engine’s term for prose that sounds like a real human] than two adjectives.
