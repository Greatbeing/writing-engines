# A Versailles Diagnosis of One WeChat Moments Post

*Translated from the Chinese; the techniques are annotated where English cannot carry them.*

**The post**: one WeChat Moments update [Note: 朋友圈, "Moments", WeChat's feed of friends' updates].

> So annoying — the company annual party raffled first prize to me again. We already have two of the same at home, and I don't know who to give it to. Last year's is still in its box. Will somebody teach me how to decline politely?

**The core boast**: good luck, and comfortable circumstances at home. Plainly: "I won the big prize again, and I have no need of it."

**Atom placement**

| Position | Atom | Note |
|---|---|---|
| "So annoying" | Privilege as Problem | A prize written as a nuisance; the tone is set on the first line |
| "again" | In Passing | The word implies this is not the first time |
| "already have two at home" | Complaint as Boast | What is complained of is having too many |
| "last year's is still in its box" | Downplayed Numbers | "Still in its box" plays down goods already owned |
| "will somebody teach me how to decline" | Asking for Advice | The plea for help is itself the boast; nobody can really answer it |

**Versailles degree** [Note: 凡度, "Versailles-degree", the engine's 1–5 score for how well a boast is hidden; from 凡尔赛, "Versailles", internet slang for a boast dressed as a complaint]: 4 / 5. The wrapping is complete, only one thing is shown off, and it is never said outright. One point off because "two of the same" and "last year's" are a little piled up; one of the two could go.

**Plain translation**: I won first prize at the annual party, and this household has no shortage of that kind of prize.

**How to write it up to a 5**: cut "last year's is still in its box", and change "I don't know who to give it to" to "my mother says if I bring another one home she'll throw it out" — let somebody else say "too much" for you.
