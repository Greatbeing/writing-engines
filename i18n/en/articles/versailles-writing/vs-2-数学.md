# I'm Really Not Much Good at Studying

*Translated from the Chinese; the techniques are annotated where English cannot carry them.*

I have been bad at maths since I was small, and I have always been a little insecure about it.

The year of the gaokao [Note: 高考, the national college entrance examination; the maths paper is marked out of 150, so 142 is a high score], I got only 142. My brother laughed at me for the whole summer. He had got 148 in his year, and it still comes up at family dinners.

University was the same. Everyone said our department was hard, and I thought so too; every final I passed with my marks grazing 90, and the scholarship I got was only second tier. My counsellor even called me in for a talk: I kept too low a profile, he said, and I should go and share my experience with the juniors.

What was there to share? I did not even know that people take prep courses for the postgraduate exam — I had always assumed that baoyan [Note: 保研, "recommended for postgraduate study": admission without sitting the entrance exam, granted to the top few in a class] just meant filling in a form.

Today my supervisor messaged again, asking whether I want to join his friend's lab abroad for a summer project. He says they wanted me because they had read my paper.

I have not decided. My English is just about enough for meetings with foreigners, and my accent is thick anyway.

Never mind. I'm going to sleep. There's a group meeting tomorrow.
