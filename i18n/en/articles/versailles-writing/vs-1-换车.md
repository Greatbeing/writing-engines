# Parking Is So Hard

*Translated from the Chinese; the techniques are annotated where English cannot carry them.*

I really did not want a new car.

The old one was small and drove perfectly well. It slipped down the alleys, and a parallel park went in at the first try. My husband insisted that running the elevated expressway every day was unsafe, and last week, while I was away on a business trip, he sold the old one outright.

The new one has three rows of seats, and its bonnet is taller than I am. This morning I drove it to the office for the first time. It would not go into an ordinary space in the underground garage — over the line on the left, over the line on the right. The security guard came over to help, studied it for a long while, and said: "Jie [Note: 姐, "sis" — the familiar address a stranger uses for a woman a little older than himself; here it also means he has decided she is somebody], this one has to go in the VIP bay." I said I was not a VIP. He did not believe me.

In the end it went into the VIP bay. The owner of the silver car beside it got out, walked a full circle around mine, and asked whether I had just taken delivery. I said I didn't know; my husband bought it; I haven't even looked at the trim list.

At home I complained to my husband. He said, then let's trade it for a smaller one and keep this for weekends.

I said, could you ask my opinion first.

He said, if I ask you, you say no.

Sigh. Sisters [Note: 姐妹们, "sisters" — the standard opening of a post in a women's forum; it presumes a crowd of friends the writer has never met], how do you all park a big car? Is there a space where you don't have to reverse? Waiting online, quite urgent.

*Sample draft: the characters are fictional, and the piece is a composite.*
*Atom chain: Privilege as Problem → Complaint as Boast → Praise by Proxy → Brands Dropped Lightly → Asking for Advice.*
*Core boast, said plainly: my husband got me a very big new car.*
