# What Gets Replaced Is Not the Person, but the Step That Stays Where It Is

*Translated from the Chinese; the techniques are annotated where English cannot carry them.*

Lately "will AI take my job" has become a splinter under a great many people's skin. A new model ships, and someone's first reaction is not curiosity but a tightening in the chest. That anxiety is neither shameful nor mawkish. A craft built up over ten years can be caught in seconds by a tool, and anyone would lie awake over it.

But it is worth asking the question differently: what AI takes — is it "the job," or one way of doing the job?

## Twelve People, Four Left

Concept artist Yi Lan is thirty-one this year. Her company brought in generative tools; the team went from twelve to four. She stayed, at thirty per cent less pay. Summing it up, she is cool about it: "I wasn't replaced by AI. I was replaced by a colleague who knows how to use AI. That distinction matters."

It does matter. It says the replacement does not happen between a person and a machine but between a person and a person — between those willing to learn and those who ran out of time to.

## A Machine Won't Tell You Which Batch Is Bad

On another production line, Zou Jianguo, a supervisor of forty-four, sees the other side. Two robotic arms arrived on the shop floor. He did not cut staff; he moved four workers to quality inspection. "A machine doesn't get tired, but a machine also won't tell you which batch of material has a problem. In the end it is still people."

What technology takes is usually the repetitive stretch; what stays is judgment, experience, responsibility. The post has not disappeared. It has shifted its position.

## Being a Little Slower Is Also a Skill

Luo Min, a teacher at a vocational school, opened a new course this term: how to give AI instructions, how to check what it returns. She puts it plainly: "I can't teach them not to be replaced. I can only teach them to be replaced a little slower."

"A little slower" is more honest than any slogan. No one can promise safety once and for all, but learning can buy time, and time can buy room to turn around.

## Catch the Anxiety, and Offer a Way Out Too

Of course, reducing all of it to "individuals must try harder" is not fair. A company cutting costs and raising efficiency cannot count only the accounts and not the people: whether there is retraining before a layoff, whether there is a transition arrangement, is a responsibility the people actually running the business cannot walk around. Vocational education has to keep up; social security has to hold the floor. That is a society's basic promise to the people who work.

AI is neither a flood-beast nor a master key. It is more like a mirror, and what it shows is whether we are prepared enough.

Anxiety is allowed, but do not let it make our decisions for us;
the tools are changing, and judgment and responsibility stay with people;
being replaced is not what is frightening — standing still and waiting is.
