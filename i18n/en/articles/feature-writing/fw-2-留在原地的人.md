# Teacher Zhou's Twentieth September

*Translated from the Chinese; the techniques are annotated where English cannot carry them.*

> Trial draft. The character "Zhou Yuanshan", the school, the students' names and all quotations are invented for demonstration; the finished piece must be built from real interviews, classroom observation and school archives [to be verified].

At 6:40 on the morning of September 1, 2026, Zhou Yuanshan pushed open the door of Grade 12, Class 3, opened the windows first, and then wiped the blackboard. In the bottom right corner of the board was a line of chalk: 278 days to the college entrance exam. He had written it the night before.

This is his twentieth year teaching at the county's No. 1 Middle School. Of the thirteen young teachers assigned there the same year he was, he is the only one still in this building.

## Assignment

Zhou Yuanshan was born in 1983. He graduated from the provincial teachers' university in 2006 and was assigned back to his home county town. On the day he reported for duty he carried a canvas trunk holding more than thirty books and a quilt.

"At the time I thought, I'll teach three years and then leave," he says.

His first cohort had sixty-three students. There was no air conditioning in the classroom; in summer he put a basin of cold water under the desk and, when his throat gave out, soaked his hand in it for a while. The detail comes from Liu Xiaomei, a student of his that year who now works at the county hospital: "By the time he'd finished soaking, the chalk dust had turned to mud."

## Those Who Left

Around 2010 colleagues began to leave, one after another. Some tested into schools in the city; some went to private schools in the south, at three times the salary.

Zhou Yuanshan took the exam once too. In 2013 the city's No. 1 Middle School was hiring; he was second in the written test, and on the day of the interview his mother was in hospital, so he did not go.

"After that I stopped taking the exams," he says. "Not because I didn't want to."

The students leave as well. He digs out a graduation yearbook: the page for the class of 2008, where the students' later cities are written — Shanghai, Shenzhen, Hangzhou, Chengdu. The page for the class of 2023 has farther places.

In his desk drawer is a stack of postcards the students have sent back, tied with a rubber band. The top one is from Ürümqi.

## Change

Zhou Yuanshan says he has changed twice.

The first time was in 2015. A boy in his class had not handed in homework for a month, and he shouted at the child in the corridor for ten minutes. Only afterwards did he learn that the boy's father had broken his leg in a fall on a building site that month.

"After that, I ask a question before I shout."

The second time was in 2020. Online classes ran for half a year, and he learned to edit video, filming himself on a phone working through formulas on the blackboard, three minutes at a stretch. In the academic office's records, the number of courseware files he submitted that year was the highest in the school.

## A Daily Timetable

Zhou Yuanshan's day begins at 5:50. He reaches the classroom at 6:40; morning reading is at 7; evening study ends at 10:10, and he stays twenty minutes more, until the last student turns off the light. His wife also teaches at the county No. 1 Middle School — English — and their two timetables are stuck on the fridge door, written in pens of different colours. Their son is in the second year of junior high this year, and only at weekends does he see a whole day of his father.

His monthly pay slip: 1,100 yuan in 2006, 6,800 yuan this year [to be verified]. He says it is less than those classmates who went south, "but it's enough; houses in a county town aren't expensive."

In 2018 a former colleague came back to the county to see him, driving a white SUV, and took him to dinner at a restaurant in the town. The colleague said they still needed maths teachers there, for the graduating class, and that the salary could reach more than two hundred thousand a year [to be verified]. Zhou Yuanshan listened, asked what time the students there finish evening study, and asked nothing more.

The colleague paid for the meal. On the way back he rode his electric bike, passed the school, and saw three floors of the teaching building still lit. He stopped at the school gate for a minute, did not go in, and rode on.

He is forty-three this year, and half his hair is white. The young teachers who have newly arrived call him "Teacher Zhou"; others call him "Old Zhou". He says either is fine.

## September

After the first period of the morning, a girl comes running to ask about a function problem. He tells her to write it on the blackboard, stands beside her and watches, watches for a long time, and says only one thing: "That step — think about it again."

The girl thinks for a moment, erases it, and writes it again.

Outside the window, on the playground, someone is raising a flag. Zhou Yuanshan puts the chalk back in its box; the box is tin, and the paint has worn off its edges. Tomorrow he will change the number of days to the exam to 277.
