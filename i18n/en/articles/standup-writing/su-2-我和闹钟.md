# Me and My Alarm Clock

*Translated from the Chinese; the techniques are annotated where English cannot carry them.*

> Subject: one performer playing both "me" and "my alarm clock" | Runtime: about 1 minute | Venue: short-video monologue
> Atom chain: Observational Opening → Act-out, rotating roles → Rule of Three → Reversal

Have you noticed — the alarm clock is the only thing in the entire household that is ever on time.

I set five of them. Ten minutes apart, one after another.

Six o'clock, 6:10, 6:20, 6:30, 6:40.

*(as the alarm clock: snaps to attention like a soldier reporting for duty, chest out, at full volume)*

"Six o'clock! Up you get!"

*(as me: face buried in the pillow, muffled)*

"Ten minutes. Just ten minutes."

*(the alarm clock, sighing)*

"You said that yesterday too."

*(me)*

"Yesterday's me is not today's me."

6:10.

*(the alarm clock, much politer this time, testing the water)*

"How about... getting up?"

*(me, eyes still closed)*

"You're an alarm clock. What would you know about a human life."

6:40. The last one.

*(the alarm clock, dropping its voice, almost gentle)*

"Bro. I'm the last one you've got."

*(me, sitting bolt upright)*

"Come on. Let's talk this through, you and me."

*(Pause)*

I turned it off.

That's the kind of person I am. I have principles.

Being late is acceptable. Being managed by an alarm clock is not.

The next morning I opened my eyes and it was half past seven.

Not one alarm went off. Not one of the five.

*(as the alarm clock: flat on its back, unhurried, deeply satisfied)*

"Yesterday's me is not today's me."

[Note: 闹钟, "alarm clock," is literally "noisy clock" — 闹 being the word for racket and fuss. The noisiest object in the house is also the most dependable one, while the human is neither, and the closing callback hands the punchline to the clock.]
