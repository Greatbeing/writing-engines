# The Annual Party

*Translated from the Chinese; the techniques are annotated where English cannot carry them.*

> Subject: a roast of the company annual party | Runtime: about 2 minutes | Venue: roast-style open mic
> Atom chain: Setup and Punchline → Mismatched Analogy → Rule of Three → Act-out → final turn on myself

Let me start with something fair.

The annual party is a good thing.

Once a year, for one evening, the company is finally willing to stand everyone dinner.

*(Pause)*

Out of everyone's year-end bonus.

What's the most moving part of the annual party?

The boss's speech.

How hard last year was, how hard this year was, how next year will be harder still.

When he finishes, everybody applauds.

We're not applauding the content. We're applauding "it's finally over."

Our company's annual party is like a wedding.

Somebody makes a speech, somebody toasts, somebody cries.

The only difference is, at a wedding, two people chose it voluntarily. [Note: 年会 is the company's annual gala; attendance is nominally 自愿, "voluntary," a word the rest of the bit makes do two jobs at once, invitation and obligation.]

The annual party act is the whole company's final exam.

Rehearsals start a month out.

Proposals by day, dance practice at night, choir on the weekend.

Why go that hard, you ask?

Because the boss said the party is "voluntary."

*(as the boss, beaming)*

"All voluntary, everyone. It's perfectly fine not to come."

*(Pause)*

"Oh — by the way, I'm the one who signs off your performance review."

Then the raffle.

First prize, a laptop.

Second prize, a phone.

Third prize, a wrap-up meeting, Monday, nine a.m.

And the first prize gets drawn by the boss's relative. Every single year.

I'm not saying anything's wrong here.

I just think his relative has very good luck. And his relative's relative has pretty good luck too.

Last, let me talk about myself.

Five years at this company. Five years performing at the annual party.

First year I danced. Second year I sang. Third year I did a skit.

Fourth year, fifth year — I played the same role.

*(Pause)*

The audience member who claps.

That's the kind of professional I am.

This year I finally won something.

Third prize.

See you Monday.
