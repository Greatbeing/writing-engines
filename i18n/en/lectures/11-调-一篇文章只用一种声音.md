# Lecture 11 · Voice: One Piece, One Voice

Some pieces make sense sentence by sentence but feel wrong as a whole: the first paragraph reads like a diary, the second like an editorial, the third suddenly turns lyrical. The problem is that the **voice** is muddled.

Voice is the person doing the talking: how old they are, who they're talking to, whether they're sitting or standing, smiling or straight-faced.

## 1. The one move this lecture teaches

> **Before you write, settle on one speaker and one listener, and don't change them for the whole piece.**

The test: **pick any two paragraphs, read them aloud, and they sound like the same person speaking on the same occasion.**

## 2. How the masters do it

Lu Xun's polemical essays are cold; his essays recalling his boyhood are warm. In "Village Opera" (《社戏》) he writes about stealing beans to eat on the way home from the opera:

> 真的，一直到现在，我实在再没有吃到那夜似的好豆，——也不再看到那夜似的好戏了。

*Translation:* Truly, to this day I have never again eaten beans as good as those that night, nor seen an opera as good as that night's.

Put this sentence in one of his polemics and it would be wrong; at the end of "Village Opera" it is exactly right. (The quotation has not been checked word for word.) **One writer can have many voices; one piece can have only one.**

## 3. What AI writes

> That day I was so tired I collapsed on the sofa, too lazy even to order takeout. At this moment I profoundly realized that the physical and mental health of contemporary youth urgently requires the high attention of all of society.

The first sentence is a chat with a friend; the second is a speech at a meeting. When readers reach the second, they'll think someone else has taken over.

## 4. One revision

**Original**
> Grandma's vegetable patch is tiny, with scallions and long beans, and it's so cute. This land has witnessed a family's perseverance and heritage and carries a deep love of the countryside.

**Breakdown**: the first sentence is a granddaughter talking; the second is a documentary voiceover.

**Revised**
> Grandma's vegetable patch is tiny, with scallions and long beans. Every morning she goes out and pulls two scallions, pats off the dirt, then stamps the holes flat. I asked her why she stamps on them. She said, "If I don't, I won't find them tomorrow."

The whole paragraph is the granddaughter talking. "Perseverance" and "heritage" need not be said; Grandma's stamping foot has said them.

## 5. Your exercise

Write about something that made you angry recently. Write it twice, about 90 words each. (The Chinese original sets 150 characters; 90 English words is roughly the same length.)

1. First version: tell it to your best friend.
2. Second version: write it as an incident report for a teacher or manager.
3. Not one sentence may be shared between the two versions.

Then compare them: same event, different listener, and the details you chose have all changed. **Voice isn't tone; it's deciding who is listening.**
