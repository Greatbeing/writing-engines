# Lecture 2 · Lines: Give Sentences Weight

Once the opening is written, most people make a second mistake: **they use adjectives in place of observation.**

"His heart was filled with complicated emotions." Nothing is wrong with this sentence, and it says nothing. "Complicated emotions" is a sack; the author has stuffed into it everything he didn't know how to write.

What does a sentence with weight look like?

> 孔乙己是站着喝酒而穿长衫的唯一的人。
> *Translation:* Kong Yiji was the only customer who drank standing up and wore a long gown.

Seventeen characters in the original, one judgment, three pieces of physical evidence: standing, drinking, the long gown. **You don't need to be told what kind of man Kong Yiji is. You've already seen him.**

## 1. The one move this lecture teaches

> **Replace every adjective with an action. If you can't, you haven't looked closely enough yet.**

An adjective is the author's attitude; an action is the reader's eyes. In writing, attitude gives way to eyes.

The test: **no more than one adjective per sentence.** More than that, and you are drawing conclusions for the reader.

## 2. How the masters do it

Lu Xun doesn't write that Kong Yiji is "poor, proud, pedantic." He writes: drinks standing up (poor; he can't afford a seat), wears a long gown (the mark of a scholar, which he won't take off). Two actions carry his status, his circumstances, and his character.

Yu Hua is sparing with adjectives when he writes about suffering. The heaviest events are often handed over in the flattest action sentences, and then the days go on. **Coldness isn't the absence of feeling. It's feeling pressed down under action.**

Wang Zengqi, writing about food, doesn't say "delicious." He writes how it's cut, how it's salted, for how many days, what is used to weigh it down. You know he ate it, because he can give the steps.

All three do the same thing: **trade invisible emotion for visible action.**

## 3. What AI writes

The machine's default move is to "upgrade the adjective": complicated → extremely complicated → indescribably complicated. It thinks adding words adds depth.

In fact, **adding words subtracts information.** After "indescribably complicated emotions," the reader has nothing in mind, because the author supplied nothing.

The second machine habit is stamping on an abstract summary:

> In that moment, he felt the weight of life.

What is "the weight of life"? How heavy? Who carries it? The sentence says nothing, yet it reads as profound. **That is exactly what makes it dangerous.** It makes people think they've read something.

## 4. One revision

**Original**
> When he learned he had failed the exam, his heart was filled with disappointment and resentment, and for a long time he could not calm down.

**Breakdown**: three emotion words (disappointment, resentment, could not calm down), zero actions. Readers can only take the author's word; they can't see for themselves.

**The fix**: delete every emotion word and keep only what the body is doing.

**Revised**
> The day the results came out, he folded his admission ticket in half, then in half again, until it wouldn't fit in his wallet, and kept it in his fist. That evening his mother called him to dinner. He said, in a minute. The minute lasted two hours.

Not one word of "disappointment and resentment." But the reader knows what those two hours were.

**This is where weight comes from**: not from more adjectives, but from **the reader walking that stretch of road himself.**

## 5. Your exercise

Rewrite the sentence below in 60–90 words. (The Chinese original sets 80–120 characters.) You may not use "nervous," "anxious," "uneasy," "apprehensive," or "sweaty palms":

> Before going on stage, he was very nervous.

**Three constraints:**

1. At least 3 consecutive actions.
2. At least 1 specific object (paper, a cup, clothing, a door handle).
3. No inner thoughts. Only what can be seen or heard from outside.

**Self-check**: show your paragraph to someone and ask, "Is this person nervous?" If they can answer, you've done it. If they ask "Why are you telling me this?", your actions aren't carrying information.
