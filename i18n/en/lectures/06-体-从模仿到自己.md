# Lecture 6 · Style: From Imitation to Your Own

Five lectures, five moves: give an image, trade for actions, stop at the scene, subtract, keep the actual words. These are method.

But what readers finally want to ask is something else: **where is my own style?**

The answer may disappoint: style isn't found, it's **imitated into being**. First you resemble one person, then two, and in the end you resemble no one.

## 1. The one move this lecture teaches

> **Pick a short piece you most want to write like, and use its skeleton to write something of your own. Copy the skeleton exactly; replace all the content.**

The test: **someone reading your imitation can tell whose skeleton it is, but not whose events.** If the events are yours, this step is done.

## 2. How the masters do it

Su Shi's "A Night Visit to Chengtian Temple" really has only five steps:

1. A time + an action (元丰六年十月十二日夜，解衣欲睡 · *Translation:* the night of the twelfth day of the tenth month, sixth year of Yuanfeng; I had undressed for bed)
2. A trigger (月色入户，欣然起行 · *Translation:* moonlight came in through the door; I got up gladly and went out)
3. Finding someone (念无与为乐者，遂至承天寺寻张怀民 · *Translation:* thinking there was no one to share the pleasure, I went to Chengtian Temple to find Zhang Huaimin)
4. An image (庭下如积水空明……盖竹柏影也 · *Translation:* the courtyard was like clear standing water… it was the shadows of bamboo and cypress)
5. One judgment, then stop (何夜无月？何处无竹柏？但少闲人如吾两人者耳 · *Translation:* What night has no moon? What place has no bamboo and cypress? It's only that there are few idle men like the two of us.)

Anyone can use this skeleton. It works for a night after overtime, the evening after an exam, the first day back in your hometown. **What can be copied is method; what can't be swapped out after copying is you.**

## 3. What AI writes

When a machine "imitates a style," it imitates the skin: for Su Shi it adds classical particles like 也 and 耳; for Lu Xun it adds "probably" and "indeed."

This is the laziest imitation and the least useful. **Vocabulary is the most superficial thing about a writer; the skeleton and the eye are what count.** Su Shi's style isn't in the particle 耳. It's in being woken by moonlight at midnight and still going out to find a friend.

To learn a style is to learn what a person **looks at, says first, and where they stop**.

## 4. One revision

**Original** (vocabulary imitating Su Shi; English rendering of a pastiche written in classical Chinese)
> On this night the moonlight was most fair; my heart was glad; thus I went out for a stroll, much moved, for life is but a dream.

**Breakdown**: the words are old, the skeleton is the machine's: conclusion first (most fair, glad), moral at the end (life is but a dream). Not one of the ailments from the first three lectures is missing.

**Revised** (skeleton imitating Su Shi, content my own)
> Eleven on Thursday night, I finished the last version of the proposal and shut the laptop. Outside, the streetlights lit the row of share bikes downstairs into a neat line. I messaged Lao Zhou. He wrote back: "Still up." We went downstairs and stood by the bikes for twenty minutes. What building has no lights on? What day has no overtime? It's only that few people will come downstairs.

Not a single archaic word. But it's Su Shi's five steps, **and it's this person's night.**

## 5. Your exercise

Use Su Shi's five steps to write one evening of your own, in 100 words or fewer. (The Chinese original sets 150 characters.)

**Three constraints:**

1. All five steps, none skipped, in the same order.
2. No archaic or literary diction. (The Chinese original bans the classical words 也, 耳, 矣, 吾, 甚; in English, avoid forms like "thus," "'tis," "most fair," "thee," "whereupon.")
3. The judgment in step five must be something only you could say: put it in Su Shi's mouth and it no longer works.

When you're done, pick a short piece by a modern writer you like, take out its skeleton, and write it again the same way. **Put the two side by side: neither skeleton is yours, but the pair of eyes that shows up in both is.**

That is where your style begins.
