# Lecture 15 · The Unsaid: Leave One Line Out

Lecture 3 said don't elevate the ending; that was about stopping in time. This lecture goes one step further: **the heaviest line, don't write it at all.**

## 1. The one move to learn in this lecture

> **Find the line the whole piece most wants to say, and delete it. Then check whether what comes before is enough for the reader to say it for themselves.**

The test: **the line that rises to the reader's lips when they finish is exactly the one you deleted.**

## 2. How the masters do it

Zhu Ziqing's "The Sight of Father's Back" is about his father seeing him onto a train. What the piece most wants to say is probably "my father loved me", and that line never appears once. What he writes is his father going across to the other platform to buy oranges:

> 我看见他戴着黑布小帽，穿着黑布大马褂，深青布棉袍，蹒跚地走到铁道边，慢慢探身下去，尚不大难。

*Translation:* I saw him in his small black cloth cap, his black cloth jacket and dark blue cotton gown, hobble to the edge of the tracks and slowly lower himself down. That part was not too hard. (Zhu Ziqing died in 1948; the text is in the public domain.)

Then he writes how hard it is for his father to climb up onto the far platform. The love is never written; a stout old man struggling up a platform is. The reader says that line themselves, and says it harder than the author could.

## 3. What AI writes

> In that moment I finally understood: a father's love is like a mountain, silent and great.

This takes the reader's line and says it for them. A reader who has been spoken for is no longer moved.

## 4. One revision

**Original**
> On graduation day, my roommate gave me his old desk lamp. I knew this was the best memento of our four years of friendship.

**Breakdown**: the second sentence is a conclusion you drew on the reader's behalf.

**Revised**
> On graduation day, my roommate gave me his old desk lamp. There was a crack in the shade from when I knocked into it while we were horsing around freshman year. He'd kept it taped up the whole time.

"Friendship" and "memento" are gone, replaced by a crack and a strip of tape. The reader thinks it for themselves: he kept that tape on for four years.

## 5. Your exercise

Take something emotional you've written. **Three steps:**

1. Circle in red the most "heartfelt" line in the piece.
2. Delete it.
3. In its place, put one concrete thing: an object, an action or a line someone actually said, so the reader reads the feeling in for themselves.

When you're done, give it to someone and ask what they want to say after reading it. If they say the line you deleted, you've passed this lecture.
