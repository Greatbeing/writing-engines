# Lecture 13 · Viewpoint: Choose Where to Stand

Season three is about the craft of the whole piece: viewpoint, rhythm, the unsaid, the turn, the scene, the draft.

Viewpoint first. The same event, seen from two people's positions, makes two different pieces. Beginners often see everything: what this person thinks, then what that person thinks, like a camera bolted to the ceiling. Everything gets filmed, and the reader has no idea whom to follow.

## 1. The one move to learn in this lecture

> **Before you start, decide where you stand: whose eyes are doing the looking. Write only what those eyes can see and those ears can hear.**

The test: **nowhere in the piece is there a line like "he thought to himself", unless "he" is the one doing the looking.**

## 2. How the masters do it

When Lu Xun wrote Kong Yiji, he didn't use the author's eyes. He used those of a twelve-year-old serving boy at the Xianheng tavern. The boy doesn't understand Kong Yiji; he just finds him funny:

> 孔乙己是站着喝酒而穿长衫的唯一的人。

*Translation:* Kong Yiji was the only customer who drank standing up yet wore a long scholar's gown.

The child misses the tragedy, and the reader takes in all of it. **The less the watcher knows, the more the reader thinks.**

## 3. What AI writes

> The mother watched her son walk away, her heart full of reluctance; the son looked back once, silently vowing to make something of himself.

One sentence climbs into two people's heads. The reader follows neither and can only stand to one side and watch.

## 4. One revision

**Original**
> Mom was very worried about me, and I was very worried about the exam.

**Breakdown**: two hearts are named, and neither is opened.

**Revised** (standing only where "I" stand)
> The night before the exam, Mom pushed the door open for the third time and said she was just checking whether the window was shut. The window was shut.

"I" can't see into Mom's heart, only see her open the door. Those three times at the door weigh more than "worried".

## 5. Your exercise

Write a family dinner in under 150 words (the Chinese original sets 200 characters; 150 English words is roughly the same length). Write it twice:

1. The first time, stand where you stand.
2. The second time, stand where the youngest child at the table stands.
3. In neither version may you write what anyone else is thinking.

Compare them: what the child sees is often what the adults never get around to writing.
