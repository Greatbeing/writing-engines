# Lecture 12 · Titles: The Title Comes Last

Many people think up a title and then write the piece, and the result is a big title over a small piece: "On Youth," "My Growth," "Gratitude." The moment the title appears, the reader knows there's nothing inside.

## 1. The one move this lecture teaches

> **When the piece is finished, take the most concrete thing in the text and make it the title: one object, one line, one action.**

The test: **the title doesn't work on anyone else's piece.** "Gratitude" works on anyone's essay, so it isn't your title.

## 2. How the masters do it

Wang Zengqi wrote about his hometown and called it "Duck Eggs at the Dragon Boat Festival" (《端午的鸭蛋》), not "Love for My Hometown." Lu Xun wrote about his childhood and called it "From the Hundred-Plant Garden to the Three-Flavor Study" (《从百草园到三味书屋》): two place names, one direction. He wrote about his teacher and called it "Mr. Fujino" (《藤野先生》): one name. Su Shi wrote about that night and called it "A Night Walk at Chengtian Temple" (《记承天寺夜游》): one place, one event.

These titles are all small, so small they can belong only to that one piece. **A big title is an empty house; a small title is a key.**

## 3. What AI writes

> "Warmth in the Depths of Time: To Those Ordinary Yet Great Moments"

An image before the colon, an elevation after it, both ends empty. This is the machine's favorite title structure, and readers spot it at a glance.

## 4. One revision

Practice on the revised drafts from earlier lectures:

| Original title | Revised |
|---|---|
| "Grandma's Love" | "The Spatula Wrapped in Blue Cloth" |
| "My Deskmate" | "The Small Triangle by the Eraser" |
| "Friendship" | "He Never Mentioned It the Whole Way" |
| "Grandma's Perseverance" | "The Stamped-Flat Scallion Holes" |

Every revised title is lifted straight from the text. When readers finish the piece and look back at the title, they feel it had already told them.

## 5. Your exercise

Bring out every exercise you have written over these twelve lectures. **Three steps:**

1. In each piece, mark the three most concrete words or the one most concrete line in the text.
2. Choose one of them as the title. You may not invent a new one.
3. No colons, no dashes, and no titles beginning with "On" or "My."

When this step is done, season two is over. Twelve exercises, twelve small titles. Look at them together: **that is the table of contents of what you wrote over these twelve weeks, and the first glimpse of your own style.**
