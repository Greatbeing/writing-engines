# Lecture 10 · Argument: Hold Every Point Up with One Event

Lecture 1 said: don't argue in the first sentence. But a piece has to say something eventually. Argumentative essays, commentary, book reports all have to make a point.

The problem isn't making points. It's making them **empty**: point after point, none of them touching the ground.

## 1. The one move this lecture teaches

> **Every point you make must be followed by one concrete event that holds it up. Better still, put the event first.**

The test: **delete the sentence stating the point; from the event alone, the reader can guess what you meant.** If they can, you chose the right event.

## 2. How the masters do it

Hu Shi wanted to say that Chinese people do things carelessly. He did not write "the national character tends toward a lack of seriousness." He wrote a person: Mr. Close Enough (差不多先生).

> 红糖白糖不是差不多吗？

*Translation:* Brown sugar, white sugar: aren't they close enough?

Mr. Close Enough buys white sugar instead of brown, writes the character 十 ("ten") as 千 ("thousand"), and at last, when ill, sends for the wrong doctor and dies. The piece has almost no commentary; the whole point is in the events.

Su Shi's "A Night Walk at Chengtian Temple" (《记承天寺夜游》) works the same way. The moonlight, the bamboo and cypress, going to find a friend come first; only at the end does it land on one line of thought: 但少闲人如吾两人者耳 (*Translation:* only there are few idle men like the two of us). **The point is the conclusion of the event, not its preface.**

## 3. What AI writes

> Perseverance is the key to success. Only by persevering can we overcome difficulties and realize our dreams. As the ancients said, keep carving without stopping, and even metal and stone can be engraved.

Three sentences, two points, one famous saying, zero events. A saying is not an event; it is someone else's point.

## 4. One revision

**Original**
> Details decide success or failure; you must be careful in everything you do.

**Breakdown**: two points stacked together. The reader nods, then forgets.

**Revised**
> Our team's report was sent back once because the table on page 7 gave its units as "ten thousand yuan" when they were actually "yuan." Every number was right, and every conclusion was wrong. Since then, before I hand in any table, I check the units first.

It never says "details decide success or failure." The reader thinks of it on their own.

## 5. Your exercise

Write a 120-word argument on a topic of your choice. (The Chinese original sets 200 characters; 120 English words is roughly the same length.) **Three constraints:**

1. At most one sentence stating a point in the whole piece, placed at the end.
2. Before the point there must be an event you experienced or witnessed, with at least one number or one line of actual speech.
3. No quoting famous sayings.

When you finish, cover the last sentence, show it to someone, and ask what you're trying to say. If they get it right, you don't need that sentence.
