# Lecture 14 · Rhythm: Alternate Long and Short Sentences

People read with their eyes, and they also hear the words in their heads. If every sentence is the same length, the piece swings like a pendulum and the reader falls asleep.

## 1. The one move to learn in this lecture

> **After each paragraph, look at sentence lengths. If three sentences in a row are about the same length, cut one short or join two into a long one.**

The test: **read it aloud. There are places where you need a breath, and places where you pause.** Put what matters most in a short sentence.

## 2. How the masters do it

The opening of Lu Xun's "Autumn Night":

> 在我的后园，可以看见墙外有两株树，一株是枣树，还有一株也是枣树。

*Translation:* In my back garden you can see two trees beyond the wall. One is a jujube tree, and the other is also a jujube tree.

The sentence drags on, goes all the way round, and both trees turn out to be jujubes. The length is deliberate: the person in that night has time on his hands, and his eyes move slowly.

Now Wang Zengqi on salted duck eggs:

> 筷子头一扎下去，吱——红油就冒出来了。

*Gloss:* the chopstick tip goes in, a squelch, and the red oil wells up.

The first half is short, the single sound word shorter still, and only the last half opens out. **Rhythm makes the reader move with your hand.** (Quotation not checked word for word.)

## 3. What AI writes

> The morning sun spilled across the land, birds sang merrily on the branches, people began their busy day, and the city slowly woke up.

Four clauses of almost equal length, each built the same way: subject, action, modifier. None of them is wrong; strung together they sound like recitation.

## 4. One revision

**Original**
> I waited at the station for a long time, the bus still hadn't come, the sky was slowly getting dark, and I began to get a little anxious.

**Breakdown**: four clauses of the same length, and the key word, "anxious", is buried at the end with no weight.

**Revised**
> I waited at the station, from five o'clock until dark, and watched the sweet-potato seller across the road pack up and wheel his stove away. The bus didn't come.

One long, one short. The long sentence pulls the time past; the short one cuts it off. "Anxious" no longer needs saying.

## 5. Your exercise

Take any paragraph you've written (at least five sentences). **Three steps:**

1. Count the words in each sentence and write the number after it.
2. Find the sentence that matters most and make it the shortest in the paragraph, six words or fewer. (The Chinese original says ten characters or fewer; six English words is the working equivalent.)
3. Make at least one sentence twice as long as before, to stretch out time or lay out a picture.

Read it aloud when you're done. If you can hear it rise and fall, you've got it.
