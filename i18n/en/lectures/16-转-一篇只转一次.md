# Lecture 16 · The Turn: One Turn per Piece

Good pieces often have one spot where the reader's heart skips. That is the turn. Beginners are afraid of being flat, so they turn constantly: happy first, then sad, then at peace with it, then reflective. The more turns there are, the less any of them feels real.

## 1. The one move to learn in this lecture

> **Give a piece only one turning point. Build up enough before it, hold still after it, and never turn a second time.**

The test: **you can point to one sentence and say: here, right here, the reader's heart sinks (or lifts).** You can point to only one.

## 2. How the masters do it

In Lu Xun's "My Old Home", the narrator returns to his village and meets Runtu, his childhood playmate. Before that comes a long stretch of memory: the melon field under the moon, the silver ring around the boy's neck, the boy spearing a badger. The reader, like the narrator, is waiting for that boy. Then Runtu speaks:

> 老爷！……

*Translation:* "Master!..."

One word. The whole story turns here, and it never looks back. The brighter the build-up, the colder this one word sounds. (Quotation not checked word for word.)

**The force of a turn comes from what is laid down before it, not from the turn itself.**

## 3. What AI writes

> At first I felt lost, but later I came to terms with it; although there were regrets, I also gained growth; even though the road ahead is unknown, I am still full of hope.

One paragraph, three turns, with "but", "although" and "even though" taking turns on stage. Each one is passed over lightly, and the reader's heart never moves once.

## 4. One revision

**Original**
> I went to see my primary school teacher, whom I hadn't seen in years. I thought she'd be delighted, but she barely recognized me. Still, we ended up having a great chat, and I made my peace with it.

**Breakdown**: it turns twice, and the second turn, "a great chat", wipes out all the chill of the first.

**Revised**
> On the way there I'd already worked out what to bring up first, something she was sure to remember: in third grade I spilled ink on her lesson plans and she made me copy out a whole notebook as punishment. I walked in. She looked at me for a long time and asked, "Whose parent are you?"

One turn only. Stop once it has turned. Don't comfort the reader.

## 5. Your exercise

Write a reunion, with a person, an object or a place, in under 200 words (the Chinese original sets 300 characters; 200 English words is roughly the same length). **Three constraints:**

1. The first two-thirds is build-up only: write how you remember it and what you expect.
2. The turn takes one sentence only, ideally a line someone actually says or a single action.
3. After the turn, write at most two more sentences, and "but", "however" and "later I came to terms with it" are not allowed.
