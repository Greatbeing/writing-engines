# Lecture 5 · Speech: Let Characters Speak for Themselves

Lecture 2 said: trade adjectives for actions. This lecture goes one step further: **trade reported speech for the actual words.**

> He angrily turned me down.

> He said, "Don't come anymore."

In the first, the author tells you he was angry. In the second, you hear it yourself, and you hear more than "angry": it might be anger, it might be hurt, he might just be tired. **The actual words hold things the author can't fully say.**

## 1. The one move this lecture teaches

> **Wherever you've written "he expressed" or "she said she was very…", replace it with the sentence the person actually said out loud. One is enough; the shorter the better.**

The test: **would this line sound wrong in someone else's mouth?** If not, you haven't written this person yet.

## 2. How the masters do it

Lu Xun, when Kong Yiji is mocked for stealing books:

> 他便涨红了脸，额上的青筋条条绽出，争辩道，「窃书不能算偷……窃书！……读书人的事，能算偷么？」
> *Translation:* His face flushed, the veins on his forehead stood out, and he protested: "Taking books can't be counted as stealing… Taking books! …The affairs of scholars, can that be called stealing?"

Lu Xun doesn't write "he cared about face" or "he was pedantic." He lets Kong Yiji open his mouth, swap "steal" for the more literary "take" (窃 for 偷), and bring out "scholars." **The moment a character speaks, he accounts for himself.**

In "My Old Home," Runtu, unseen for twenty years, stands before the narrator:

> 他的态度终于恭敬起来了，分明的叫道：「老爷！……」
> *Translation:* At last his manner became respectful, and he said clearly: "Master!…"

Two characters in the original. Before them, a childhood playmate; after them, a thick wall. The heaviest point in the whole story is one line of speech.

## 3. What AI writes

Machine dialogue has two ailments:

1. **Reporting**: "My mother earnestly told me that one must keep one's feet on the ground in life." Not one word is the mother's.
2. **Speechmaking**: a character opens their mouth and out comes a complete moral. "Child, life is like a marathon. What matters isn't the starting line, but perseverance." Nobody talks like that.

Real speech is broken, short, half-finished, beside the point. **The only one who speaks in complete morals is the author.**

## 4. One revision

**Original**
> The day I left, my father earnestly urged me to take care of myself out there, not to work too hard, that everything at home was fine and I needn't worry.

**Breakdown**: "earnestly" is the author's judgment; the four clauses after it are the author's summary of what the father meant. The father says not a word.

**Revised**
> The day I left, my father carried my suitcase to the station and didn't say anything the whole way. When the bus was about to pull out, he tapped on the window: "Call when you get there."

One line of actual speech, and an ordinary one. **Ordinary is what makes it him.** The urging, the worry, the reluctance: readers hear them on their own.

## 5. Your exercise

Write a scene of 70–100 words (the Chinese original sets 100–150 characters): two people, one small matter (borrowing something, waiting for someone, an argument).

**Three constraints:**

1. At least two lines of direct speech, each no longer than 8 words. (The Chinese original sets 12 characters.)
2. No adverbs or phrases dressing up "said" ("earnestly," "excitedly said," "replied helplessly"). Use only "said," or no tag at all.
3. The two people's lines can't be swapped: swapped, they no longer work.

**Self-check**: copy the dialogue out on its own and read it aloud. **If it sounds like a script or a speech, shorten it. If it sounds like something you overheard yesterday, it's right.**
