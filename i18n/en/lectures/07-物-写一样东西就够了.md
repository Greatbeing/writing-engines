# Lecture 7 · Things: One Object Is Enough

Season one taught you to write sentences. Season two teaches the larger materials of a piece: things, people, order, argument, voice, title.

Things first. When beginners write about a setting, a hometown or an era, they reach for the **panorama**: green hills, clear water, simple folk, tidy streets. Anyone can write a panorama, so no one remembers one.

## 1. The one move this lecture teaches

> **To write a place, write only one thing from it, and write it all the way through.**

The test: **when readers close the piece, they can say what that thing looks like, how it is used, and who uses it.** If they can, the place stands up.

## 2. How the masters do it

Wang Zengqi wrote about his hometown, Gaoyou. He skipped the Grand Canal and the old town and wrote about a salted duck egg:

> 筷子头一扎下去，吱——红油就冒出来了。

*Gloss:* You push the tip of a chopstick in, and with a hiss the red oil wells up.

Then he wrote how to choose an egg ("一要挑淡青壳的" — *Gloss:* first, pick one with a pale green shell), how children hang eggs on their chests, and how the empty shell becomes a firefly lantern. One egg got over a thousand characters, and Gaoyou is inside it.

**Things are easier to write than scenery, because things have uses.** A use brings a person, and a person brings a life.

## 3. What AI writes

> My hometown is a beautiful small city with a long history, unique food and warm-hearted people, and every corner is full of everyday life.

History, food, people, corners: four things, none of them written. "Full of everyday life" is a conclusion; the reader never smelled anything.

## 4. One revision

**Original**
> My grandmother's kitchen was full of warm memories.

**Breakdown**: "warm" and "memories" are both conclusions. What is in the kitchen? Not one thing is given.

**Revised**
> By my grandmother's stove hung an iron spatula, its handle wrapped three times in blue cloth cut from her old apron. When she cooked she didn't look at the wok, she looked at me, and the spatula turned in the wok by itself.

Only a spatula. The blue cloth is hers, the hand turning it is hers, the eyes on me are hers.

## 5. Your exercise

Write about the place you grew up, in no more than 180 words. (The Chinese original sets 300 characters; 180 English words is roughly the same length.) **Three constraints:**

1. Only one main thing may appear in the whole piece.
2. You must show one way it is used (who uses it, and how).
3. You may not use "beautiful", "warm", "memories" or "everyday life" (or "cozy", "nostalgic").

When you finish, ask yourself: if someone else wrote about their hometown, would this thing be exactly the same? If so, pick something narrower.
